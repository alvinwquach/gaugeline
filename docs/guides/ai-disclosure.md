# Form or chat input sent to an AI service

A question box on a dental practice's page, a "help me choose" assistant on a shop's, a chat on a coach's site:
many of them hand what a visitor types to a model run by another company. People type different things to a
person than to a machine, and many want to know who else sees it. `ai-input-no-disclosure` finds the pages where
input goes to an AI service and the page does not say so.

Gaugeline does not say what a law requires where a business operates. That is a question for the client and their
own counsel. This check only tells you what the page says and does not say, and telling visitors is cheap.

It reads the page and the same-site script files the run already read, and asks for nothing else, so it needs no
consent flag.

## An example: a dental practice's question box

Juniper Street Dental is an invented practice. Its page has a question box whose script sends the text to a model
provider's address. Nothing on the page says an AI answers.

```sh title=Terminal
$ npx gaugeline juniper-street-dental.example
```

```text title=Output
! Visitors can type to an AI service and the page does not say so
    a script on / sends what a visitor types to api.openai.com, and nothing on the page says an AI answers
? 4 checks could not run: email-spf-missing, email-spf-multiple, email-dmarc-missing, email-dmarc-none
    Mail records are read from DNS only with --dns. Rerun with --dns to check them.

1 to look at, none to fix first.
```

Each check that passed also prints a `✓ <id>` line, left out above. The finding names where the input goes. It
never prints page text, a field or a query string.

The fixed copy of the page says, next to the box, "Answers here are written by an AI assistant from our published
opening hours, and may be wrong", and the finding goes.

## What it looks for

Three kinds of flow, all from the page itself:

- **A form** whose `action` is the API host of a model provider (a fixed list: Cohere, DeepSeek, Fireworks AI,
  Google Gemini, Groq, Hugging Face, Mistral, OpenAI, OpenRouter, Perplexity, Replicate, Together AI and xAI).
- **An AI assistant widget** the page loads, from the vendors table behind
  [Chat and AI widgets on your pages](ai-widgets.md) (the ones shown there as "AI assistant").
- **A script** that writes one of those hosts as an address, on a page that has a text box to type into. Inline
  scripts and up to 15 same-site script files are read.

A disclosure is any of a fixed list of phrases in the page's visible text, a placeholder, an `aria-label`, a
`title` or an image `alt`: AI, artificial intelligence, chatbot, virtual or automated assistant, language model,
generated by an AI, powered by a named model, bot. Text in scripts, styles, templates and comments is not read,
because a visitor never sees it. The list is generous on purpose: a page is flagged only when none of it is there.

What it does not see: a flow built at run time from pieces of an address, one on another page, one inside a
widget's own frame, and an assistant the site runs through its own server route (a route that talks to a model
looks like any other route from the page; `ai-endpoint-open` asks the ones you list). The provider and vendor
hosts are from memory, not from their own pages, which could not be reached when the check was written: the check's
technical line in the [checks reference](/docs/checks) says so.

## If you find one

1. Say it in plain words next to the box or in its placeholder: "Answers here come from an AI assistant and may be
   wrong."
2. Check that the privacy notice says what visitors type is sent to the AI provider, and what the provider does
   with it. The client and their own counsel decide the wording.
3. If the box should not send input to a third party at all, move the call behind the site's own server and look at
   what that route logs.
